First Principles & The Latent Manifold

I did not enter scientific computing to collect credentials, court academic prestige, or participate in institutional status games. When I moved from my village to Delhi for college, I realized very quickly that the conventional lecture-hall curriculum was designed for passive compliance rather than raw capability. I chose a different path: intense, self-directed mathematical research driven entirely by first principles, leveraging modern AI as an unconstrained thinking partner to confront the deepest bottlenecks in computational physics.

Over the past year, that self-directed discipline has yielded 10 independent research frameworks across inverse problems, theoretical fluid dynamics, and symplectic celestial mechanics—with manuscripts under active review at journals such as IEEE Transactions on Computational Imaging, Physics of Fluids, and Applied Mathematical Modelling.

To me, intellectual mastery cannot be detached from physical reality. Working the land with heavy machinery in my village, sketching continuous curves and contours with pencil on paper, testing bodily limits through relentless daily conditioning, and deriving high-dimensional autograd Jacobians in PyTorch are not disparate pursuits. They are different projections of a single invariant: the mastery of difficult things.

Operator Conditioning and False Convergence in Incompressible PINNs

Proved that continuous mesh-free PINNs lack discrete stencils for Thom's wall-vorticity condition, causing False Convergence and Operator Diffusion in streamfunction-vorticity ($\psi$--$\omega$) models ($67.3\times$ residual drop while underpredicting secondary corner eddies by $14.3\%$). Field Notes & Sovereignty

On Capability vs. Credentials: In computational mathematics and engineering, code and theorems are incorruptible. A simulation either diverges or it conserves physical energy; an autodiff graph either blows up VRAM or it executes in 35 milliseconds. Credentials, recommendation letters, and titles are social artifacts. True capability is an absolute invariant.

On the Rhythm of Hard Work: When working on difficult mathematical derivations, hitting a cognitive wall is guaranteed. My answer is to step away from screens—go back to my village, work in the fields, drive the tractor, or push physical exertion until the body is exhausted. When grounded in real earth and physical sweat, mental knots untangle naturally.

On Open Science: I have zero interest in secretive research or hoarding scripts behind "upon acceptance" excuses. Every pipeline I formulate is released openly on GitHub and archived with persistent DOIs on Zenodo. When your mathematics is sound and your code runs, you have nothing to hide and no gatekeeper to fear.
